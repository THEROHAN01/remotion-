turtleauth

I built turtleauth from scratch as a production-ready authentication and identity service: sessions, JWTs, OAuth/OIDC, multi-factor authentication, passkeys (WebAuthn), and role-based access control, all in one reusable layer other apps can plug into instead of gluing together yet another set of auth libraries.

I approached it with a security-first mindset throughout — careful session handling, rate limiting, and an RBAC model designed to be explicit rather than bolted on. Getting these details right, and getting them right consistently, was the whole point of the project.

I built it solo, on purpose, so I'd understand authentication systems end-to-end instead of only ever wiring up an off-the-shelf provider. It's still evolving as I find more edge cases worth handling properly.

jarvis

I built jarvis, production-grade retrieval infrastructure for building accurate, scalable, enterprise-ready RAG systems. It covers the parts of retrieval that are easy to get wrong: chunking documents sensibly, generating and indexing embeddings, and tuning retrieval quality so the right context actually reaches the model.

The goal was to move past toy RAG demos toward something that holds up at enterprise scale, where document volume, latency, and retrieval accuracy all matter at once.

crowd-vibe

I built crowd-vibe as an experiment in sensing the mood of a crowd or event in real time — a small exploration of how a group's shared energy at a gathering might be captured and reflected back, rather than a finished product with a fixed feature set.

It's still evolving, and the exact direction is something I'm continuing to figure out as I build.

brainly-monorepo

I built brainly-monorepo, a "Brainly"-style knowledge and bookmarking app, structured as a monorepo with the frontend and backend living side by side. The idea was a simple, personal way to save and organize links, notes, and ideas you want to come back to.

Building it as a monorepo let me iterate on the client and API together without the overhead of juggling separate repos while the shape of the product was still changing.

lockedin

I built lockedin, an execution operating system that transforms long-term goals into daily missions. It's a personal productivity tool aimed at closing the gap between having a goal and actually doing something about it today.

The core idea is breaking down anything you're working toward into small, concrete daily actions, so progress is something you can see and track instead of something you hope is happening in the background.
